## Beyond Folders and Rules

For decades, email organization has relied on the same primitives: folders, labels, and rule-based filters. You create a rule that says "if subject contains 'invoice', move to Finance folder." It works — until it doesn't.

The problem with rules is that they're **brittle**. Real business emails don't follow templates:

- "Can you take a look at the attached and let me know if we're good to proceed?" — Is this an approval request? A review request? Feedback?
- "Quick update on the Henderson project" — Is this a status report? A risk escalation? A request for resources?

Humans understand the *intent* behind these emails instantly. Rules don't.

## How AI Classification Works

Modern AI email classification uses Large Language Models (LLMs) to understand email content the way humans do — by reading the full text and understanding context, not just matching keywords.

### Step 1: Content Analysis

The AI reads the email subject, body, and metadata (sender, recipients, thread context). It considers:

- **Linguistic cues**: "Please approve", "here's my weekly update", "we have a problem"
- **Structural patterns**: Bullet points with status items, attached documents, forwarded chains
- **Sender context**: Is this from a known vendor? An internal team member? A client?

### Step 2: Intent Classification

Based on its understanding, the AI assigns the email to a business category:

- **Approval** — Someone is requesting a decision
- **Report** — Someone is sharing status or progress
- **Issue** — Someone is raising a problem or blocker
- **Feedback** — Someone is providing input or review comments
- **Informational** — FYI only, no action needed

Each classification comes with a **confidence score** — a measure of how certain the AI is about its judgment.

### Step 3: Field Extraction

Beyond classification, the AI extracts structured fields relevant to each category:

- For approvals: requester, amount, deadline, urgency
- For reports: milestones completed, blockers, next steps, owner
- For issues: severity, affected system, proposed resolution

## Why Accuracy Matters More Than You Think

The difference between 90% and 98.5% accuracy might seem small, but in practice it's enormous.

### The Math

If you receive 100 emails per day:

- **90% accuracy** = 10 misclassified emails per day = **50 per week**
- **98.5% accuracy** = 1.5 misclassified emails per day = **~8 per week**

That's a 6x difference in errors. And each misclassified email means a missed approval, an overlooked blocker, or an ignored deadline.

### How Flowmail Achieves 98.5%

Flowmail uses a **two-pass classification strategy**:

1. **Fast pass**: A lightweight model quickly classifies the email based on subject and the first 500 characters. This handles the ~85% of emails that are clearly one category.

2. **Deep read**: For emails where the fast pass confidence is below the threshold, a more powerful model reads the full email body (up to 2,000 characters) with a detailed prompt that considers context, tone, and structural cues.

This approach balances speed (most emails classified in under 1 second) with accuracy (ambiguous emails get the attention they deserve).
