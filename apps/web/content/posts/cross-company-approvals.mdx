## The Approval Black Hole

You send a budget approval request to your client's CFO on Monday. By Wednesday, you're wondering: did they see it? Is it stuck with someone else? Should you follow up or wait?

This is the **approval black hole** — and it's one of the most common pain points in cross-company collaboration. When approvals span organizational boundaries, things get complicated fast:

- **Different email platforms**: Your team uses Gmail, the client uses Outlook, the legal team uses something else entirely.
- **No shared task system**: You can't assign a task to someone in another company's Jira.
- **Lost context**: The approval request is in one thread, the supporting documents are in another, and the conversation about changes is in a third.

## What a Typical Cross-Company Approval Looks Like

Let's trace a real-world example: a vendor submitting a quarterly budget for client approval.

1. **Vendor PM** sends a budget proposal email to **Client Finance**
2. **Client Finance** forwards it to **Client CFO** for review
3. **Client CFO** asks a question — replies to Client Finance (not the vendor)
4. **Client Finance** relays the question back to the Vendor PM
5. **Vendor PM** responds with updated numbers
6. **Client CFO** approves — but the approval email gets buried
7. **Vendor PM** follows up a week later, not knowing it was already approved

**Total time wasted: 5-7 business days** for something that should take 24 hours.

## How to Fix It

### 1. Structure Every Approval Request

Instead of free-text emails, every approval should have structured fields:

- **Requester** and their organization
- **Amount** or scope of what's being approved
- **Deadline** for the approval decision
- **Current status** (pending, approved, rejected)

Flowmail's AI automatically extracts these fields from incoming emails, so you don't need to change how people send requests.

### 2. Track Status Across Organizations

The key insight is that **approval status should live outside of any single email thread**. When an approval is extracted as a structured entity, you can:

- See all pending approvals in one dashboard
- Track which ones are approaching their deadline
- Know exactly who needs to take action next

### 3. Automate Follow-ups

When an approval approaches its deadline without a response, an automatic reminder should go out — not from a person, but from the system. This removes the awkwardness of manual follow-ups and ensures nothing slips.

### 4. Close the Loop Automatically

When an approval is granted or rejected, everyone who needs to know should be notified immediately:

- The requester gets a confirmation
- The finance team updates their records
- A calendar event is created for the next milestone

## Real Results

Teams using structured approval tracking report:

- **60% faster** approval cycle times
- **Zero** missed approval deadlines
- **80% reduction** in follow-up emails
